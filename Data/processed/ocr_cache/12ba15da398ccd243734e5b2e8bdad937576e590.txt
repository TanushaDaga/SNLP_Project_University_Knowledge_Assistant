Course Objective
2. Highlight the dynamic role of society, culture and politics in the field of science & technology
especially post artificial intelligence.
Course Outcomes
After completion of the course, students will be able to-
1.
. Understand the complex relationship between science, technology and society.
2. Develop an interdisciplinary understanding of scientific knowledge and technological
production.
3.
 Evaluate empirical evidences and theoretical claims in science, technology and society along
with policy matters.
4.
 Analyze the policy matters and technical governance for artificial intelligence, vaccine
development, stem cells and nanotechnology.
Detailed Syllabus
Unit
Description
Duration
1.
Introduction of science and technology
10
Definition, scope and interrelationship of science, technology and society,
Inductivism versus hypothesism, structure of scientific & industrial revolution,
emerging scientific community, rights and wrongs of science, cognitive,
hierarchical & ethical dimensions of science.
2.
Technology & Media
10
Science fiction and films, philosophy of technology, role of media, technology
and character of everyday life, Humans versus computers, Langdon Winner's
theory on autonomous technology, technology as a shadow constitution, net
neutrality, inequalities in technology.
3.
Social shaping of technology
10
Social perspectives of modern science, open science and social media, science
as a social institution, responsibilities of scientist, Kuhn and paradigm shifts,
Feyerabend and problem of autonomy, Demarcation in contemporary
scientific methods.
4.
Science, Technology and Law
Karl Popper's theory, controversies regarding science and technology, science,
technology and the less-developed countries, technology and transformation of
work; Science, technology and economic theory, stem cells, vaccines and animal
experimentation.
